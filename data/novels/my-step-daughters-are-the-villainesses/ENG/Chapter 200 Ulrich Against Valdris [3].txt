Frightening.

That was the only word Valdris could find to describe what Ulrich was just doing right now.

Frightening.

He had heard stories about Ulrich van Rubenhart before coming to Skargardia.

A young nobleman who lost his mother young. A genius expected to become one of the Kingdom’s notable foreign mages one day. Then, according to the reports Valdris had received, Ulrich’s mana core had blossomed broken. His potential had become useless. His future had narrowed. He had withdrawn from the world and later reappeared as an obscure Count following his father’s death.

Valdris had expected a man bitter about what he had lost.

He had expected a mage who clung to basic spells because he could not afford anything greater.

He had not expected this.

The genius was real.

Valdris could no longer deny it.

The manner in which Ulrich controlled mana, formed runes, and changed spell structures in the middle of combat belonged to a genius. No ordinary mage could rebuild a formation so quickly while defending against an opponent of Valdris’s strength.

But the broken mana core?

That part made no sense.

It could not be real.

A crippled core could not produce mana with such speed. It could not sustain repeated spell formations with this level of control. It could not allow a man to weave, destroy, and rebuild reinforcement spells while fighting with every part of his body.

Valdris’s eyes narrowed.

"Your mana core is not crippled," he said.

Ulrich did not reply.

"You cannot perform magic at this speed with a damaged core," he continued. "It is impossible. The reports were false."

Still Ulrich gave him nothing.

His silence answered more than words would have.

Valdris’s smile returned slowly.

Perhaps his informants had been mistaken. Perhaps Ulrich had hidden his true condition deliberately. A nobleman with enough influence could conceal much if he wished. Or perhaps the Count had found a way to repair the damage and had kept the matter secret from everyone around him.

Whatever the answer, Valdris would no longer treat him as a weakened mage.

"You leave me with few choices, Count Rubenhart," he said.

Ulrich lifted his sword slightly.

Crimson runes continued to form around it.

Valdris jumped backward, creating distance between them.

Ulrich did not pursue immediately. He watched the commander land near the far bank of the stream, boots sinking into the soft earth. Valdris’s black coat hung damp around his legs, and the cut on his cheek had begun to bleed more freely.

Then Valdris turned his head toward the black-armored knights scattered throughout the camp.

"Take the serum," he ordered.

The Valmorian soldiers reacted at once.

They broke away from their immediate exchanges with the Rubenhart knights. Some stepped backward with shields raised. Others used sudden bursts of force to create room, shoving their opponents away with the shoulders or striking hard against their blades.

Hendrick noticed the movement.

"Do not let them regroup!" He shouted.

Two Rubenhart knights tried to press forward, but the Valmorian soldiers had already reached inside their armor.

Small glass vials appeared in black-gloved hands.

Each one contained a dark liquid, nearly black beneath the firelight. The substance clung thickly to the glass when the soldiers moved it. A faint red glow stirred at the bottom of the vials, dull and unpleasant, like embers buried beneath ash.

Ulrich’s gaze hardened.

The Valmorian knights removed the stoppers.
