The sisters followed their escort through the long corridors of the royal castle, their steps echoing softly over polished stone.

The closer they came to the outer gate, the more the atmosphere changed.

The private, muted quiet of the royal family’s wing gave way to the organized movement of a castle preparing for a noble’s departure. Servants carried trunks down staircases. Stable hands crossed the courtyard with horses in tow. Knights spoke in low voices as they checked straps, weapons, and the seals placed upon the Rubenhart luggage.

Then Esther saw the light beyond the open doors.

"Outside," she whispered.

The three sisters stepped into the courtyard.

The royal gates stood open before them.

Beyond the gates lay the capital’s broad streets, still watched closely by royal guards after the demonic attack.

The Rubenhart carriage waited at the center of it all.

Four horses stood at the front, their breath faintly visible in the cooling evening air.

Hendrick waited near the carriage.

He stood with more than a dozen Rubenhart knights mounted on horseback.

And Ulrich was there.

He stood beside the carriage door, dressed for travel.

His formal clothing from the ceremony had been replaced by a fitted black traveling coat that fell to his knees, worn over a dark shirt and sturdy boots. His gloves were back on his hands. A crimson scarf had been wrapped at his throat, its color matching the carriage and the banners attached to the knights’ lances.

He looked almost entirely recovered.

The faint weariness remained around his eyes if one looked closely enough, but he stood straight.

He was not alone.

Princess Camellia stood before him.

She had come to see them off.

She wore a pale cream cloak over a light rose-colored dress, the fabric falling softly around her figure. Her platinum-pink hair had been tied back with a ribbon, and though she carried herself with the natural elegance of a royal princess, she did not look like she had come merely to fulfill a courtly duty.

She had come because she wanted to.

Ulrich appeared to be finishing a quiet conversation with her. He inclined his head as Camellia spoke, while she held one hand lightly over the small pouch at her side.

The pouch that held the pendant.

Esther saw her first.

"Princess Camellia!"

The sound burst from her before Hermione could stop her.

Esther hurried across the courtyard, blue skirts lifting slightly around her feet. Her face brightened so completely that it seemed impossible she had been crying only the day before.

Camellia turned.

The moment she saw Esther approaching, her expression softened into a smile.

"Esther."

Esther stopped before her, breathing a little faster from the sudden rush.

"We were just leaving," she said, as though the waiting carriage, mounted knights, and open castle gates had not already made that obvious. "I thought you were inside with Her Majesty."

"I was," Camellia replied. "But I wanted to come down before you departed."

Hermione and Airam arrived a moment later.

"Your Highness," Hermione said. "There was no need for you to come all the way here."

Camellia shook her head.

"I know there was not."

Her gaze moved between the three sisters.

"But I wished to see my friends off."

Esther’s eyes widened.

Then she beamed.

"You called us your friends."

"Of course I did."

Camellia’s answer was simple, but it warmed Esther more than any grand declaration could have.
