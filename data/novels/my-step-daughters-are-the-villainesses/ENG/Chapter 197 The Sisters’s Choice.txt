"They are not yours to take."

He said, releasing his mana; his blade flared into a crimson light once again.

"They are mine."

Valdris’s expression cooled.

"You have no right over them," he said. "They are royalty. They bear the blood of an Emperor and the blood of one of the mightiest witches this age has known. You know nothing of their heritage, their potential, or the responsibilities attached to them."

His gaze shifted briefly toward the sisters.

"Do you truly believe you can raise them properly? You cannot guide them toward their rightful future when you do not even understand the world they were born into."

"I took them in knowing what they were capable of," Ulrich replied.

His eyes never left Valdris.

"Each of them possesses talent beyond anything most mages will ever have. Airam, Hermione, Esther, every one of them has power that could grow beyond the limits people believe possible."

Crimson mana continued to spread around his sword. It hissed softly against the damp night air, casting blood-red reflections across the stream.

"But power in the wrong hands becomes a weapon," Ulrich continued. "It becomes an excuse for people to decide a child’s worth before she is old enough to decide anything for herself. That is why Anna-Maria took them away from Valmoris. That is why she separated them from the covens."

Valdris’s lips curved upward.

"Wrong hands."

His voice held quiet amusement, though none reached his eyes.

"You expect me to believe your hands are good ones, Ulrich van Rubenhart?"

The commander took a slow step closer. His black sword dragged through the wet grass at his side, its edge leaving a narrow line in the earth.

"How much witches’ blood is on those hands?" Valdris asked. "How many people died in the village before you took those girls into your Estate? You destroyed their home. You killed the witches who lived there. You cut down the very people who had sheltered them before you came along and called yourself their guardian."

Ulrich did not answer.

"They will never be safe at your side," Valdris said.

He turned his attention to Airam.

"Princess Airam."

His tone changed when he spoke her name. It became gentler, almost patient, as if he were addressing a frightened child rather than a girl who had just rejected him.

"You are old enough to understand this. You have seen the truth of Skargardia. You know what this kingdom does to witches. You know what men like him did to your people."

Airam’s face remained hard, but she listened.

Valdris raised one hand toward her.

"The enemy is standing before you," he said. "The man who killed countless of your kind. The man who helped destroy the life you once had. The man whose actions led you deeper into danger and left your mother dead."

Hermione’s eyes lowered.

Esther looked at Ulrich, then at the black-armored commander. She did not seem to understand every word, but she understood enough. Her fingers began trembling around the fold of Airam’s nightgown.

Valdris extended his hand farther.

"Come forward," he said. "Your father is waiting in Valmoris. Emperor Sedhoran wants his daughters home. He wants to see you. He wants to protect you. You and your sisters will have a place beside him, where none of this suffering can reach you."

For a moment, Airam did not move.

The campfire snapped behind Ulrich. Somewhere to the left, steel crashed together, followed by the sound of a Rubenhart knight falling against the side of a supply carriage. Hendrick shouted for the remaining soldiers to hold their formation.
