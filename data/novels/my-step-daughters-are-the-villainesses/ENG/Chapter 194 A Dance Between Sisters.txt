Supper had ended some while before, the last of the broth scraped clean from wooden bowls and the fire allowed to settle into embers, when Ulrich told them plainly that they ought to retire to the tent if they hoped for proper rest before the early start awaiting them at dawn. Yet the three of them lingered instead, unwilling to trade the last of the evening for sleep so soon after eating.

Or rather, it was Hermione who insisted on staying, since she understood better than her sisters what waited for them once they crossed back within the Estate’s walls: long days confined to its halls and gardens, lessons stacked upon lessons, and precious little cause to feel open sky above their heads or grass beneath their boots.

She did not fault Ulrich for the shape their lives would take. He watched over the three of them with a caution that had only sharpened since Libra’s attack, and she understood the thoughts behind that vigilance even when it chafed against her. Still, she wanted to wring a little more from the evening while she could, especially with the landscape spread so handsomely around them, the stream running silver-dark under the rising moon, the tall grass silvered with dew, the mountains beyond holding the last violet light of the sunset against their peaks.

Ulrich did not press the matter further. He allowed them their small stretch of freedom, settling instead near the fire with his sword within reach, watching them without watching them, the way he always did.

"Once we’re back, it’s straight into lessons again," Hermione said, letting out a long breath through her nose.

"I’m so happy to see Monika, Linnea and the others again," Esther said, bouncing where she sat.

"I’m only interested in what more the Grandmaster has to teach me," Hermione said.

"At least we won’t have to endure dance lessons with Elana anymore," Airam said from the side.

Hermione turned toward her, a slow smirk spreading across her face.

"You must be terribly disappointed, then, Airam."

"No."

"But eldest sister," Esther chimed in, tilting her head, "you looked like you were enjoying yourself so very much when you danced with Lord Ulrich."

"I did not enjoy it. It was exhausting," Airam said, folding her arms.

"Exhausting, and yet you managed such elegant footwork with him. Isn’t that right, Esther?" Hermione rose to her feet and pulled Esther up with her by the wrist. "You looked positively absorbed in it."

She dropped into an exaggerated approximation of a court bow, then began sweeping Esther through the same steps, prompting a fit of giggling from the younger girl as she stumbled to keep pace.

"And with such a spectacular finish," Hermione added, twisting Esther through the final pose Airam had struck in Ulrich’s arms: the one where he had held her suspended nearly parallel to the floor, her hair black spilling downward. Hermione reenacted it with herself standing in for Ulrich and Esther playing Airam, dipping her sister low with theatrical flourish.
