Hermione stepped away from Airam and Esther, giving her elder sister room. Esther remained behind Airam, holding tightly to the back of her nightgown. She watched Hermione with wide, wet eyes.

Hermione raised her hand.

A Formula Ring appeared above her palm.

It was small.

Only two rings.

The first held a pure mana formation. The second contained simple control and release patterns. Hermione did not add elemental runes. She did not build a projectile or a barrier. Instead, she prepared a compact force spell, light enough that it could be formed quickly and directed without striking the Rubenhart knights.

The ring turned once.

Then it split.

Three smaller circles appeared around it.

Hermione controlled each one separately.

Airam glanced toward her.

"You can manage three."

"Obviously. I do not need them to be strong," Hermione said. "I only need them to interfere."

The first small Formula Ring released.

A burst of force struck the Valmorian knight fighting Hendrick from the side.

It did not send him staggering.

The pulse struck the man’s elbow at the moment he began his sword swing, but the black armor absorbed much of the impact. The serum-enhanced soldier’s arm only shifted by a fraction. His blade still came down toward Hendrick’s shoulder.

Hendrick barely turned in time.

His own reinforcement spell flashed across his armor and body, a thin golden layer of mana woven tightly around his muscles and joints. The black sword struck his shoulder plate. The anti-magic effect tore through the enhancement at the point of contact, scattering the spell into faint sparks, but it had held long enough to prevent the blade from cutting fully through.

Metal screeched.

Blood ran from a split beneath Hendrick’s collar.

He stepped backward rather than falling.

The opening Hermione had created was small.

Hendrick used it anyway.

He drove his sword toward the Valmorian knight’s side. The enemy twisted, and Hendrick’s blade struck black armor rather than flesh. Sparks jumped from the impact. The armor dented under the force but did not break.

The Valmorian soldier answered with an elbow toward Hendrick’s face.

Hendrick blocked with his forearm.

The impact made his vision blur.

A second enemy came at him from the left with a spear. Hendrick turned, his sword barely catching the shaft before the point reached his throat. He shoved the spear aside and stepped away from the swordsman’s next strike.

Two against one.

Before the serum, Hendrick could have handled them.

Now he was being forced backward.

The Valmorian soldiers moved too quickly. Their black armor resisted his attempts to cut them down. Their strength made every blocked strike painful. They attacked with the discipline of soldiers who had trained together for years, one forcing Hendrick’s attention forward while the other searched for his blind side.

Hermione saw it.

Her second small Formula Ring released.

A narrow force pulse struck the spearhead.

The weapon did not fly from the Valmorian knight’s hand. The anti-magic reinforcement built into the spear resisted the spell, and the soldier’s grip did not weaken. Still, the pulse turned the point enough that it missed Hendrick’s chest and scraped across his wounded shoulder instead.

Hendrick grunted.

He seized the shaft beneath the spearhead.

The Valmorian knight pulled back.

Hendrick held on.

For a moment, neither man moved. The serum gave the Valmorian soldier immense strength, but Hendrick’s reinforcement spell remained active across his arms. Golden mana wrapped around his muscles, flickering each time the enemy’s force threatened to break his grip.

The swordsman came in.

Hendrick released the spear.

The sudden loss of resistance threw the Valmorian knight half a step backward. Hendrick ducked beneath the swordsman’s cut and slammed the pommel of his sword into the side of the spear wielder’s helmet.

Once.

The soldier’s head snapped sideways.

Hendrick struck again.

The second impact dented the black metal near the ear.

But the knight remained standing.

The serum had made him harder to put down as well as stronger. The man turned back toward Hendrick with a low, furious sound.

Hermione’s third spell-circle hovered above her fingers.

She looked toward the Valmorian swordsman closest to the tent.

The knight had been waiting for Hendrick to divide his attention. He saw the captain struggling against the spear wielder and moved in from the side, sword raised.

Hermione pointed.

The force spell released.

It struck behind the knight’s knee.

The armor absorbed most of the impact.

His leg did not buckle.

He did not fall.

But his step landed half a pace shorter than he intended. His sword reached Hendrick too early, before the knight had fully positioned his body behind the strike.
