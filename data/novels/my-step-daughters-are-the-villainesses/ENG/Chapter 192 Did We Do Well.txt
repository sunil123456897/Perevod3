The Rubenhart carriage had been traveling for nearly three hours.

By then, the capital was far behind them.

The stone roads closest to Skargardia’s royal castle had gradually given way to quieter routes bordered by dark woods, open fields, and low hills. The wheels rolled steadily beneath the carriage, their rhythm muted by the thick interior walls. Outside, the mounted Rubenhart knights moved in disciplined formation around them, their armor occasionally catching the pale glow of the moon.

They had departed in the evening.

If the road remained clear and the weather did not turn against them, they would reach Rubenhart territory by the following afternoon.

Inside the carriage, the arrangement had remained the same as it had been during their journey to the capital.

Ulrich sat alone on one bench.

Across from him sat the three sisters.

Esther occupied the middle, tucked warmly between Hermione and Airam, her hands folded in her lap. Hermione sat beside the window with her cloak drawn around her shoulders, while Airam leaned against the opposite side, her dark eyes watching the night beyond the glass.

Yet the mood inside the carriage had changed entirely.

On their way to the capital, the sisters had been tense.

Esther had been excited but nervous, imagining royal ballrooms, princesses, gowns, and all the things that might happen within the walls of the grand castle. Hermione had tried to hide her worry behind a composed expression, though she had watched every noble carriage they passed as if expecting someone to stop them and demand an explanation for why three witches were traveling with Count Rubenhart.

Airam had been silent.

Not nervous, exactly, but vigilant.

She had watched the roads, the guards, the servants at every inn, and the shifting expressions of every stranger who noticed the Rubenhart crest.

Now, on the way home, they looked different.

Not entirely carefree.

The ceremony had taken too much from them for that. They had seen the cruelty of the court. They had heard the whispers. They had watched demons fall dead upon a royal ballroom floor. They had watched Ulrich bleed, collapse, and lie feverish beneath the care of royal physicians.

But they had endured it.

They had danced in a royal ballroom despite the stares. They had spoken to a princess. They had received kindness from the Queen. They had offered gifts that Camellia had truly cherished.

And they had returned to the carriage not as frightened girls hoping to survive an unfamiliar court, but as members of House Rubenhart who had faced it and remained standing.

That had been one of Ulrich’s reasons for bringing them.

The noble world was not gentle.

It was beautiful from a distance: bright chandeliers, silk gowns, gold-rimmed dishes, music, and graceful dances. But beneath the elegance lived ambition, prejudice, cruelty, and people who smiled while searching for a weakness to exploit.

Ulrich wanted the sisters to understand it.

Not so they would become cold.

But so they would not be broken by it.

Esther, especially, had entered the capital carrying too many bright dreams. She had expected royal festivities to resemble the stories she liked to read. She had expected every noble to behave like a courteous prince or graceful lady.

Instead, she had learned that beautiful and noble people could be cruel.
