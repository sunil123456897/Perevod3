The camp had settled into a quieter rhythm by the time dinner was served.

The Rubenhart knights had arranged a makeshift dining area near the center of the clearing, far enough from the stream that the ground remained dry and stable. Thick fallen logs had been placed around the fire, with broad planks resting over two of them to create simple benches. It was hardly comparable to the polished dining hall of the royal castle, but the knights had done their best to make it comfortable.

A large fire burned at the center.

Its flames rose and curled against the dark sky, sending sparks upward toward the stars. The heat reached the sisters where they sat together on one of the benches, warming their hands and faces against the cool night air. Behind them, tents had been set up in orderly rows: a large command tent near the carriage, several smaller tents for the knights, and a separate one for the three sisters.

The smell of dinner drifted through the camp.

The meal was simple by royal standards, yet warm and satisfying. Thick stew filled with tender meat and root vegetables had been ladled into wooden bowls. Fresh bread had been warmed beside the fire, and small cups of sweet herbal tea had been prepared to ward off the evening chill.

Esther held her bowl carefully in both hands.

Steam rose against her face as she blew on the stew before taking a bite.

"It is good," she declared.

Airam looked at her.

"You have not even swallowed it yet."

"I know it is good already," she giggled.

Hermione sat beside Esther, tearing a small piece from her bread. Seeing a firefly passing by, she smiled a little.

"You should let it cool first," she said. "You will burn your tongue, Esther."

"I am being careful, big sister."

Esther took another bite.

Then she immediately made a small sound and covered her mouth.

Hermione sighed.

"You are not being careful."

Airam reached for her cup of tea and pushed it nearer.

"Drink."

Esther obeyed.

Across the fire, several Rubenhart knights ate in quiet conversation. Their voices stayed low, but the sisters could hear occasional laughter mixed with the clink of bowls and the soft stamping of horses nearby. Others remained at their posts, moving along the edge of the clearing or standing watch on the small rise behind the camp.

The firelight could not reach the entire field.

Beyond the tents and lanterns, darkness waited in every direction.

Esther turned in her seat.

Her eyes searched the camp until she found Ulrich.

He was not near the fire.

He stood at the far edge of the clearing, where the open grass met the distant tree line. His black traveling coat moved faintly in the night breeze as he walked in a slow circle around the perimeter. One gloved hand was extended before him, fingers moving with precise gestures.

Crimson mana glowed beneath his fingertips.

It spilled into the grass in thin lines, faint at first, then brighter as the spell settled into place. The light traveled through the ground like a thread of red fire, forming runes that flashed once before sinking beneath the soil.

Ulrich continued onward.

He did not rush.

He paused every few steps to inspect the mana structure, sometimes lowering his hand close to the earth, sometimes drawing a new rune into the air. The barrier he was creating would surround the camp in several layers: a warning spell to alert him if something entered the perimeter, a deterrent against ordinary beasts, and a defensive layer strong enough to delay anyone foolish enough to approach under cover of darkness.
