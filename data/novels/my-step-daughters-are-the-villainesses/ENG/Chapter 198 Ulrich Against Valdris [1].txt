The blades met again.

The sound rang across the camp, sharper than the clash of the knights fighting around them. Ulrich’s crimson-covered sword struck Valdris’s black blade, and the red runes burst apart at once. Flecks of mana scattered through the air before vanishing above the stream.

Ulrich did not waste time watching them disappear.

He stepped inside Valdris’s reach.

Most swordsmen would have moved away after losing a spell at the point of contact. Valdris’s sword was made to break magic, and every mage who had fought him before likely learned that lesson quickly. They would retreat, create space, and try to cast a larger spell from a safer distance.

Ulrich did the opposite.

He drove his shoulder toward Valdris’s chest, forcing him to shift backward, and brought his sword upward from below. The strike aimed beneath Valdris’s guard, toward the space between the black blade and his ribs.

Valdris turned his wrist.

Black steel caught Ulrich’s sword before it could reach him.

Ulrich’s arm shook from the impact. His fingers numbed around the hilt. Valdris’s strength traveled through their locked blades with frightening force, and he used it to shove Ulrich back.

Ulrich allowed himself to move with the pressure rather than fighting it directly. His boots slid through the wet grass. He twisted his body, released the crossed position of their swords, and cut toward Valdris’s thigh as he regained his footing.

Valdris drew his leg back.

The edge passed close enough to slice the lower hem of his black coat.

He answered with a downward strike aimed at Ulrich’s collarbone.

Ulrich raised his sword.

Their weapons collided.

Clang!

The runes gathered over Ulrich’s blade shattered under the touch of Valdris’s weapon. The crimson light vanished. Yet Ulrich’s sword remained in place, and he guided the force of Valdris’s strike away from his body. He moved his hands with the blow, changing the angle at the last moment. The black blade slid across his steel and struck empty air beside him.

Valdris immediately attacked again.

He moved faster this time.

His sword came from the left, then the right, changing direction before Ulrich could fully settle his stance. The black blade cut through the night in clean, brutal lines. It carried no wasted motion. Each strike had a purpose: force Ulrich backward, narrow his choices, destroy his balance, and open his body for a final cut.

Ulrich retreated three steps.

Not because he was losing control.

Because Valdris wanted him to retreat four.

The fourth step would have brought Ulrich onto a patch of uneven ground near the stream, where loose stones lay beneath the grass. Valdris had noticed it, and Ulrich noticed that Valdris had noticed it.

Instead of moving back, Ulrich pivoted.

Valdris’s next strike passed where Ulrich’s neck had been a moment earlier.

Ulrich turned around the attack and stepped to the commander’s outside. His sword came toward Valdris’s back.

Valdris reacted instantly. He spun, catching the attack with the lower portion of his blade. Their swords scraped together, black against crimson, and Valdris used the collision to bring his elbow toward Ulrich’s face.

Ulrich leaned away.

The elbow passed before his nose.

He answered with a short kick to Valdris’s knee.

Valdris lifted his leg, avoiding the strike by a narrow margin, and the two men separated.

For a brief instant, they stood facing one another beside the stream.

The firelight reached them in broken orange flickers. Crimson sparks still drifted from the remains of Ulrich’s destroyed reinforcement spells. The water behind Valdris moved over pale stones, disturbed by the commander’s earlier landing. Around them, the camp had become a battlefield. Rubenhart knights fought desperately to keep the Valmorian soldiers away from the tent. The black-armored warriors pressed forward in a disciplined formation, their anti-magic weapons forcing Ulrich’s men into close combat.

Yet the fight between Ulrich and Valdris drew the eyes of everyone near enough to see it.

Hermione stood near the tent entrance with one hand clasped around Esther’s. Her fear had not disappeared. She could hear the cries of the wounded, see the blood on the grass, and feel the danger closing around them.
