"Are you having a good trip, Count Rubenhart?"

Ulrich held the stranger’s gaze without a word. The fire behind him cast a low red sheen across his eyes, turning their natural color darker, deeper, until they appeared almost black at the edges.

The man remained smiling beneath that stare.

"My apologies," he said. "I failed to introduce myself when we met at the ballroom." He placed one hand over his chest and bowed slightly. "Valdris Nedomian. War-Mage Commander in service to the Valmorian Empire."

"Valmoris," Ulrich said, narrowing his eyes.

Far beyond Skargardia’s east lay the Valmorian Empire: a large and disciplined human dominion that had raised its cities, roads, and armies through generations of conquest. Its nobility did not inherit security simply because of blood. Strength earned rank there, retained it, and took it from those who proved too weak to keep it. Their soldiers trained from childhood. Their mages served their legions as weapons of war. Their craftsmen forged arms and armor whose quality few kingdoms could equal.

Ulrich knew enough of Valmoris to understand that a commander of its war-mages did not wander through foreign lands without purpose.

He did not know why Valdris had come for the sisters.

"Leave," Ulrich said.

The threat in his voice did not need embellishment.

Valdris let out a small laugh, though his eyes did not leave Ulrich’s face.

Boots disturbed the grass outside the crimson barrier.

Figures emerged from the darkness one after another. Their armor was blackened metal, fitted close over their bodies and stripped of every unnecessary ornament. Moonlight caught along ridged pauldrons, hooked helmets, and the edges of long weapons carried at their sides. They formed a loose line beyond the barrier, their movements ordered, their armor producing a restrained clatter whenever one shifted position.

"My lord!"

Hendrick’s voice cut across the camp.

Rubenhart knights rose from their places around the fire. Swords came free of scabbards. A few moved toward the large tent at once, placing themselves between the sleeping sisters and the border of Ulrich’s barrier. Others fanned out across the clearing, watching the unfamiliar soldiers through the faint crimson shimmer.

"There is no need for bloodshed, Count Rubenhart," Valdris said. "This matter may yet end without it."

"What do you want?"

Valdris’s smile returned, broader this time.

"Only the three young witches under your protection."

"No."

Ulrich answered before the last word had properly left Valdris’s mouth.

Valdris’s expression remained composed, but his hand lowered from his chest.

"I am aware that you took them in knowing what they are," Valdris said. "I am also aware of their talent. Yet talent requires proper cultivation. Those girls possess the potential to become far more than servants hidden behind a mere Count’s walls, or weapons trained in the customs of a kingdom that fears witchcraft whenever it cannot control it."

His dark gaze drifted past Ulrich toward the distant tent.

"They do not belong here. Skargardia will never offer them what Valmoris can. In my Empire, their magic would be taught, sharpened, and given purpose. They would stand among people who understand its value."

"You have said enough," Ulrich replied. "You will not take them."

Valdris watched him for a few seconds. The pleasantness left his face by degrees, replaced by open disappointment.

"That is unfortunate."

He raised his hand.

A sword appeared in his grasp.
