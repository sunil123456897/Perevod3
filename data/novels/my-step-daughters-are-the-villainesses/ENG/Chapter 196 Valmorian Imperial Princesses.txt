"Princess Airam. Princess Hermione. Princess Esther," Valdris said. "It has been a long time."

For several seconds, none of the sisters answered him.

Airam’s body had gone frozen. The softness that had remained on her face only moments ago, when she had watched Esther and Hermione dancing near the stream, vanished at once. Her eyes fixed on Valdris’s face, and she took a small step backward without seeming to realize it.

Hermione looked no better.

The color had left her cheeks. Her hands, which had been resting at her sides when she came from the tent, curled tightly into the thin fabric of her nightgown. She stared at Valdris as if she had seen a dead man walk out from the dark beyond the campfire.

Then both of them looked around once again.

They saw the black-armored knights who had crossed the ruined perimeter of Ulrich’s ward. They saw the Rubenhart men and women holding their formation near the tents and the stream, their swords drawn, their faces set with grim concentration. They saw the bodies already lying in the trampled grass.

The sight did not calm them. It only drove the shock deeper.

"P–Princess?" Esther asked.

Esther had not been looking at Valdris. She had been looking from Airam to Hermione, waiting for either of her sisters to say something, to tell her that the strange man was confused, that he had mistaken them for someone else.

Neither did.

Her gaze drifted toward the closest fallen Rubenhart knight.

The man lay near the edge of the firelight, one arm thrown across his chest. His armor had been pierced beneath the ribs. Blood soaked into the grass around him, dark enough to look black under the night sky.

Esther drew in a frightened breath.

"Eeek!"

She rushed behind Airam and pressed herself against her eldest sister’s back. Her small hands clutched at the loose folds of Airam’s nightgown as she buried her face in Airam’s back. Airam immediately reached behind her, catching Esther’s wrist and holding it there. The gesture was protective, though her fingers trembled.

Ulrich watched the girls closely.

"Do you know him?" He asked.

His sword remained raised, its crimson mana still crawling across the blade in broken lines where Valdris’s weapon had damaged the reinforcement spell. He did not turn fully toward the sisters. Doing so would have exposed his back to the Valmorian commander.

Airam did not answer.

Ulrich’s eyes narrowed.

When Valdris first appeared near the Estate’s ballroom, Ulrich had assumed the man knew the sisters because of Anna-Maria. The former Matriarch had been famous enough that anyone involved with witches, organizations, or foreign noble courts might have recognized her daughters. Valdris’s interest had been suspicious, certainly, but it had still seemed explainable.

Now the man had called them princesses.

Not young ladies. Not daughters of a respected witch. Not heirs to a coven.

Princesses.

The title did not fit Anna-Maria’s status, however powerful she had once been. A Coven Matriarch might command witches, noble families, mercenaries, or creatures. She could even influence the politics of an entire kingdom. But this was not it.

There had to be another reason.

"T–That’s..." Hermione tried to say.

Her voice failed.

She swallowed, though the movement looked painful. Her eyes kept returning to Valdris, as if she feared that looking away would make him come closer.

Airam’s hand tightened around Esther’s wrist.

"What are you doing here?" She asked.

Her voice was cold, but Ulrich could hear the strain beneath it. It was very rare to hear her like that.

Valdris’s expression brightened.

"What am I doing here?" He repeated, almost amused. "I have come to bring you and your sisters back to where you belong, Princess."

"We do not belong there."

Airam’s response came quickly.

"Mother warned you," she continued. "She warned all of you to leave us alone. She told you never to follow us, never to approach us, and never to involve us in anything connected to Valmoris or the covens again."

For the first time, Valdris’s smile weakened.

"Anna-Maria did make that request," he said. "A request, I should remind you. Not an order she had the authority to enforce against the Valmorian throne."

"She was our mother."

"And she was a powerful woman," Valdris replied. "No one denies it. That is precisely why her wishes were respected while she lived."

His gaze moved from Airam to Hermione, then to Esther, who still hid behind Airam.

"We stayed away. We allowed Anna-Maria to keep you beyond the Empire. We accepted her decision to raise you where she wished, among people she trusted. Yet Anna-Maria is gone. Her protection is gone with her. You are no longer under her care."

"You have no right to speak of her."

"I speak of reality," Valdris said. "Whether you hate me for it or not, you three are alone now. You have no mother. No coven stands behind you. No powerful guardian remains who can prevent the Empire from claiming what is its own."
