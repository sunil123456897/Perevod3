Valdris narrowed his eyes.

’He needs to die.’

Ulrich had become far more dangerous than Valdris had expected. His swordsmanship had already surpassed his first assessment, and the Count’s mana control made the situation worse. He used magic with the speed of a battle-hardened mage, yet he did not rely on large spells or dramatic displays. Every formation served a purpose. Every spell bought him an opening, protected a weakness, or forced Valdris to give up ground.

The crimson reinforcement around Ulrich’s blade burst apart.

Valdris’s black sword cut through it, dismantling the mana structure with a shower of red sparks. Yet the strengthened connections within the spell resisted long enough to slow the commander’s strike.

Ulrich shifted aside.

The black blade passed across the front of his coat, slicing cloth rather than flesh.

Ulrich answered with a thrust toward Valdris’s throat.

Valdris knocked it aside, twisting his sword sharply enough that Ulrich’s blade passed beside his shoulder. At the same time, his left hand rose.

A Formula Ring opened in the air beside him.

Its rings turned rapidly, glowing with the deep blue-white light of concentrated fire mana. The spell took shape faster than most trained mages could manage. Valdris had selected a fire formation suited for pursuit rather than a single explosive blow. Two long lances of flame formed over his shoulder, narrow enough to move quickly and hot enough to cut through armor if they struck cleanly.

Ulrich saw the final control pattern settle into place.

The fire lances would not chase him immediately.

They would pass him first.

Then they might turn.

Valdris released the spell.

The two streams of flame shot forward.

They passed Ulrich on either side, close enough for heat to bite through his clothes. Their first path carried them away from him, streaking through the steam and smoke above the stream.

Then they curved.

One came for his back.

The other turned sharply toward his chest.

Ulrich’s left hand rose.

A smaller Formula Ring appeared beside him, pale and silver. He did not try to build a heavy defensive spell. There was no time for that. Instead, he formed two compressed planes of wind and released them at the exact moment the fire lances closed in.

The first wall of air struck the flame pursuing his back.

The lance bent away from him, dragged sideways by the sudden force of the wind. The second plane caught the other lance as it crossed toward his chest and forced it upward.

Both attacks missed.

One slammed into a tree beyond the camp.

The trunk burst apart under the impact. Fire climbed the exposed wood at once, and burning bark rained across the grass. A thick branch crashed near the outer edge of the tent. One of the Rubenhart knights rushed over and kicked it away before flames could catch the canvas.
