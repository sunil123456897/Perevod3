"We need to find out," Ulrich said.

"We," Kaliantha repeated.

The word lingered between them.

She sat across from him with one leg crossed over the other, her posture flawless despite the private nature of the meeting.

"You have a poor image at court, Lord Rubenhart," she said. "Most people see only the cold Count who avoids their company, speaks little, and appears to care for nothing beyond his own territory."

Ulrich did not dispute it.

"Nobody knows how extensively you have assisted this kingdom," Kaliantha continued. "They do not know how many demon ships have been stopped because of you. How many lives were spared. How many of their own estates may have been protected by information you provided in secret."

Her eyes narrowed slightly.

"And it seems you have no wish for anyone to know."

"To be respectful, Your Majesty," Ulrich said, a faint coldness entering his voice, "I could not care less for other people’s acknowledgment."

Kaliantha watched him.

"What about mine?"

Ulrich raised an eyebrow.

The question was unexpected.

Kaliantha did not look away.

"Should I assume that you care what I think of you?" She asked, her expression serious.

Ulrich considered the question.

Then he gave a small nod.

"Your Majesty is the Queen."

For a brief moment, Kaliantha’s lips curved into a faint smile.

It was not quite amusement.

"You truly are difficult," she said.

Ulrich remained silent.

"Two years," Kaliantha said. "For two years, you have helped me more than most people realize. You have given me warnings that saved lives. You have quietly acted against threats to my kingdom. And when demons came for me directly, you put yourself between me and them without hesitation."

Her gaze lowered to her hands.

"But I know you are hiding something from me."

The faint warmth in her expression disappeared.

"It prevents me from judging you as I wish to judge you. I cannot give you my complete trust while I know there are truths you deliberately keep from me."

Her fingers tightened slightly together.

"Even if I wish to."

Ulrich looked at her.

Kaliantha’s honesty was more direct than he expected.

She did not pretend to trust him completely.

She did not demand he explain himself immediately, either.

She was simply telling him where they stood.

And the fact that she wanted to trust him mattered more than she likely understood.

"All Your Majesty needs to know," Ulrich said slowly, "is that I am on your side."

Kaliantha’s eyes lifted.

"Not the kingdom’s?"

Ulrich did not hesitate.

"Your Majesty is the kingdom."

Silence followed.

Kaliantha’s expression remained composed, but something shifted in her mismatched eyes. Ulrich could not name it. Surprise, perhaps. Or something softer that she did not permit herself to show for long.

"Is that why you were so willing to protect me?" She asked. "Why you put your life on the line?"

Ulrich’s gaze shifted slightly.

"Your Majesty possessed the necklace," he began. "I was relieved to know that—"

"It does not matter."

Kaliantha interrupted him.

"The necklace can protect me. It may resist poison or prevent a single lethal blow. But it cannot fight five demons. It cannot remove me from a sealed barrier. And no one but you knew precisely what it could do."

She leaned forward slightly.

"Yet you remained beside me."

Ulrich looked back at her.

"You knew there was danger. You had every opportunity to retreat. Instead, you stood between me and those demons. You took their attacks. You bled for me."

She narrowed her eyes.

"Why?"

Ulrich did not answer.

The truth was complicated.

He had not truly believed his own death was possible to begin with. He was confident in fighting off and surviving.

Kaliantha, however, was different.

The Phoenix Heart might have protected her.

Or it might not have.

The demons could have prepared for it. They might have possessed a way to suppress it, delay it, or exploit the moment before it awakened. And even if it activated successfully, its existence would be exposed. Once the demons knew of the Phoenix Heart, they would not allow it to remain an unknown advantage.

Ulrich wanted to delay that discovery for as long as possible.

But that was not the entire truth either.

He had seen Kaliantha in danger.

And he had moved.

She was far too important a figure, one of the rare level-headed figures in this world with actual power and status, capable of making things happen.

"Because—"

"I do not expect you to say, ’because you are my Queen,’" Kaliantha interrupted.
