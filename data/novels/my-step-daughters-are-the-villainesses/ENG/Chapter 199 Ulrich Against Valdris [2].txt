Clang!

The collision of their swords rang across the camp.

Ulrich and Valdris resumed their fight beside the stream, their boots cutting through the wet grass as they circled one another. Crimson light flashed whenever Ulrich’s blade met Valdris’s black sword. The red spell coating Ulrich’s weapon shattered at every contact, scattering sparks across the ground, yet the exchange no longer looked like the one-sided battle it had been at the beginning.

Earlier, Ulrich had been forced to defend.

Valdris had driven him backward with the force of his swings. Every attempt Ulrich made to reinforce his blade had ended the same way: black steel struck crimson mana, the spell broke, and the impact numbed his hands.

Now the ground between them had become equal.

Valdris’s expression no longer held amusement.

His smile had disappeared completely.

He swung from the right.

Ulrich’s sword rose to meet it.

The black blade struck the crimson reinforcement spell.

Red sparks scattered across the darkness.

The spell shattered.

Yet Valdris’s sword did not pass through it as easily as before.

He felt the difference immediately.

The resistance lasted only an instant. It was not enough to stop his sword. It was not enough to keep Ulrich’s spell intact. The black blade still severed the formation, breaking through the layers of mana and destroying the runes connected across Ulrich’s steel.

But the spell forced Valdris to put more strength into the strike.

That tiny delay mattered.

Ulrich’s crimson light vanished from the blade, leaving bare steel behind. Before Valdris could turn the momentum of his broken swing into another attack, Ulrich had already moved his sword away, created a sliver of distance, and drawn fresh mana through his hand.

Crimson runes formed again.

The same structure returned.

The runes did not simply circle the sword in a thin layer. They were bound together in clusters. Each connection had been strengthened with additional mana, turning the reinforcement spell into a denser formation. The lines between the runes had become thick enough to endure the anti-magic effect for a brief moment before finally tearing apart.

Valdris attacked again.

Clang!

The crimson spell broke.

Ulrich rebuilt it.

Clang!

The spell broke again.

Ulrich rebuilt it again.

Each time, the structure appeared with the same accuracy.

Each time, Valdris’s sword had to cut through the reinforced connections before it could fully destroy the spell.

Valdris’s eyes narrowed.

Ulrich was not the first person to understand how an anti-magic blade worked.

Valmorian knights trained with such weapons from the beginning of their military education. Their instructors repeated the same warnings year after year: an anti-magic weapon was powerful, but it was not invincible. It could disrupt spells. It could tear through weak formations. It could give a trained swordsman the advantage against a mage who relied too heavily on mana.

It could not erase magic from the world.

A large enough spell would overwhelm the blade’s ability to dismantle it. A complex enough formation could resist long enough to inflict damage. A mage with enough mana, enough preparation, and enough understanding of spell construction could create a force the sword could not fully disperse.

That was why the Valmorian knights never believed themselves untouchable.

They learned to close the distance quickly.

They learned to pressure mages before stronger spells could be completed.

They learned to force their enemy into close combat, where the advantage of the anti-magic blade became decisive. A mage could stand far away and cast elaborate formations, but that took time. A mage forced into a sword fight had little opportunity to build high-rank spells.

Valdris had expected exactly that from Ulrich.

He had known that Ulrich’s mana core was damaged. The Count possessed skill, knowledge, and unusually refined control, but he lacked the overwhelming reserves a powerful mage would need to cast great spells repeatedly. Valdris had assumed that breaking Ulrich’s initial reinforcement would force him into a pure martial exchange.

That part had happened.
