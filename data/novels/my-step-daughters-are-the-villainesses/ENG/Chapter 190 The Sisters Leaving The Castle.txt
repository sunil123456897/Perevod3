By late afternoon, the three sisters had returned to the chamber they had shared within the royal castle.

The room no longer looked as though three girls had lived there for several days.

Outside the tall windows, the day was slowly descending toward evening.

Their departure was approaching.

The sisters’ traveling clothes had already been prepared.

They were not as extravagant as the gowns they had worn during Camellia’s coming-of-age celebration, obviously, but Ulrich had not permitted anything plain or cheaply made to be packed for them either. Their dresses were practical for the journey, warm, layered, and made to withstand the cold air that would settle after sunset, but every stitch still carried the refinement expected of ladies from House Rubenhart.

Hermione stood near the mirror in a deep burgundy traveling gown. The high collar framed her pale neck, while the dark cloak resting over her shoulders was fastened with a small silver clasp. The dress was elegant without being ostentatious, the color suiting her serious expression and silver hair.

Airam wore a midnight-blue gown with long sleeves and a narrow belt at the waist. It bore almost no ornament beyond thin silver embroidery at the cuffs and hem. She had chosen it herself, declaring that excessive lace would only become caught on branches, carriage doors, and people’s hands.

Esther’s gown was pale blue.

It was softer in color than either of her sisters’ dresses, with white fur lining at the collar and cuffs. A small silver brooch held her cloak in place, and the fabric of her skirt moved lightly whenever she shifted from one foot to the other.

Yet Esther was not admiring herself in the mirror.

She knelt beside a wooden case placed on the carpet near the bed.

The box was crimson, with the red crest of House Rubenhart engraved discreetly into its lid. It was open now, revealing the dress Esther had worn to the royal ceremony.

The gown had been folded perfectly.

Soft white cloth had been placed between the layers to protect the fabric, while the jewelry she had worn was arranged in separate velvet-lined compartments beside it. Her necklace lay coiled carefully in one section. A bracelet rested beside it. The earrings had been placed in a small round case so they would not be crushed during the journey.

Esther looked down at them with a smile.

She reached into the box and gently patted the folded gown.

"It was such a beautiful dress," she said.

A shame, she wouldn’t wear it anytime soon or perhaps not anymore ever as she would eventually grow up. In the end, Ulrich had spent so much for a single night; Esther only hoped, despite how it began, they made a good impression.

Hermione glanced toward her from the mirror.

"It is still a beautiful dress."

"I know." Esther’s smile widened. "I just mean... I cannot believe Lord Ulrich had it prepared for us, big sister. It looked like something a real lady would wear."

"You are a lady," Hermione said.

Esther tilted her head.

"A witch lady?"

"A lady who is also a witch."

"That sounds better," she giggled.

"It is more accurate."

Esther returned her attention to the pale fabric. Her fingers hovered over the edge of the gown, careful not to disturb the folding.

"I liked dancing in it," she said quietly. "I liked when Lord Ulrich held my hand and told me not to care what the others said."

Hermione’s expression changed in the mirror.
