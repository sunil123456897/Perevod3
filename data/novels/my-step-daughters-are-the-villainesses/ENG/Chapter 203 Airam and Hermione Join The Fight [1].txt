The camp had become a nightmare.

Valdris’s attack had turned the quiet clearing beside the stream into a place of fire, broken armor, and death. Only a short while ago, the Rubenhart knights had been resting around the campfire. Some had been speaking in low voices. Some had been checking their weapons before sleep. Others had watched the three sisters dance beneath the moonlight with quiet amusement.

Now the grass was torn beneath armored boots.

The tents had been scorched by stray fire spells. The supply carriage burned near its wheel, sending smoke through the clearing. The stream reflected crimson light from Ulrich’s magic, orange light from Valdris’s flames, and darker stains that spread across the water where the dead had fallen close to its banks.

The Rubenhart knights had faced a difficult battle from the beginning.

Valdris’s soldiers carried anti-magic swords and armor made to resist spells. The usual advantage of a knight who could reinforce a blade, bind an enemy’s movement, or strike with elemental magic had been stripped away. Every time a Rubenhart knight tried to weave a Formula Ring, the black weapons cut through the spell before it could become decisive.

Then the Valmorian soldiers drank the serum.

After that, the battle became impossible.

Their strength increased. Their speed rose. Their bodies moved with an unnatural force that ordinary men could not match. A Rubenhart knight who might have held one opponent back before now struggled to survive against the same enemy. A single careless exchange could send someone flying. A sword strike that should have been blocked could break through a guard and tear into armor.

One by one, the Rubenhart knights fell.

Airam, Hermione, and Esther had kept their eyes on Ulrich for as long as they could.

They had watched him fight Valdris beside the stream. They had watched him defend against flames, swords, and spells while bleeding from his side. They had watched crimson mana flare around his blade again and again, only for the black anti-magic sword to shatter it.

But the battle around them could no longer be ignored.

Hermione saw a familiar knight collapse beside the campfire.

She did not know whether it was a sword or a spear that struck him. The darkness hid the worst of it. For that, she was grateful. The night covered the ground in shadow and spared her from seeing every detail of the blood spreading beneath bodies.

It could not hide the sounds.

The screams reached them.

The clash of weapons.

The dull impact of armored bodies falling into the mud.

The tearing noise when a blade found a gap between plates.

The harsh breaths of people trying to remain standing after they had already been wounded too badly.

Worst of all were the voices.

They belonged to people the sisters knew.

Not well enough to call family, perhaps, but well enough that their deaths did not feel distant.

The female knights who had helped them prepare for bed were dead.

Hermione remembered one of them fixing Esther’s hair just earlier while the other just wished them good nights with a kind smile.

The cook who had prepared their meals during the journey lay near the supply carriage. His wooden spoon had fallen beside him. Esther had thanked him after supper. He had smiled and told her that she should eat more if she wanted to grow strong.
